The Limits of AI:Geospatial Foundation Models
Do Not Understand Geography

Geospatial foundation models can identify surface objects and spatial patterns.
But spatial representation is not the same as geographic understanding.

Here, “do not understand” means that current models still lack robust, scale-aware and mechanism-oriented geographic reasoning.

01 / OBSERVATION

Models first learn observational representations.
Not geographic mechanisms themselves.

Imagery, maps and trajectories are observations of the world.
They contain geographic information, but are conditioned by sensors, scale and sampling.

Recognizing spatial patterns
is not the same as explaining the processes that produced them.

MOVE / Pixels provide observational evidence; process, relation and history still require inference.

02 / SPATIAL GENERALIZATION

The real test is whether a model
generalizes across regions.

Random splits often leave training and test samples in similar spatial environments and can overstate performance.
Spatial holdout is closer to deployment: the model must face unseen regions and distribution shift.

The key geographic capability
is robust inference across places.

EarthShift reports roughly 15–20% average degradation under real spatial distribution shifts. See Sources.

03 / PLACE & SCALE

Coordinates locate an object.
They do not define a place.

Place is constituted by location, scale, spatial relations, environmental processes and history. Geographic claims are therefore scale- and context-dependent.

Change the scale,
and relations, dominant processes and valid conclusions can change.

DRAG SCALE / The same spatial object participates in different relation structures at different scales.

04 / SPACE ≠ GEOGRAPHY

Spatial representation
is not geographic understanding.

3D reconstruction and viewpoint transformation describe geometric consistency.
Geographic explanation also requires regional differentiation, process mechanisms and spatial dependence.

A spatial model answers where things are and what geometry they have.
Geographic intelligence must also explain why they occur here and why places differ.

MOVE / Viewpoint changes geometric representation; it does not automatically explain the mechanisms of place.
