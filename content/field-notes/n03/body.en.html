My previous note introduced a reviewer skill built from remote-sensing papers in Nature journals. It quickly exposed a more practical problem: remote-sensing work that reaches a Nature-level audience is often not judged primarily as an innovation in sensing methodology. Earth observation is frequently the entry point to a broader Earth-system question involving climate, hydrology, ecology, or atmospheric processes.

That means a manuscript is unlikely to face a reviewer with only one disciplinary perspective. It is more realistic to expect a panel of different scientific priors. I therefore built a reviewer matrix for interdisciplinary geoscience: nature-earth-system-reviewer-skills.

The current set covers four areas:

☁️ Atmospheric science    💧 Hydrology    🌿 Ecoclimate    🌍 Remote sensing and Earth observation

The Earth-system collection has so far analyzed 213 public peer-review files from Nature journals, extracted roughly 15,800 reviewer-comment units, and distilled several hundred reusable review patterns.

All skills are available on GitHub: nature-earth-system-reviewer-skills. Testing, stars, and issues are welcome.

These skills are AI-assisted tools. Their review comments are advisory only; final submission decisions should follow the judgment of supervisors and collaborators and the journal's official requirements. The project has no affiliation with Nature Portfolio.
